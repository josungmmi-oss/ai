Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive multivariate analysis of Seoul's bus license status as of December 2025. The analysis examines 498 bus routes across 6 different bus types, revealing significant patterns in operational characteristics, resource allocation, and efficiency metrics.

Key Highlights

  • Total 498 bus routes analyzed across 6 types: Feeder (233), Trunk (161), Customized (57), Night (30), Express (15), and Circular (2)
  • Average fleet size: 14.8 buses per route with an average route distance of 35.8 km
  • Strong correlation (0.998) between licensed buses and operational buses indicates efficient fleet utilization
  • Significant variation in operational patterns across bus types, with trunk lines showing highest intensity
  • Customized buses maintain highest spare vehicle ratio (40.8%), while circular buses show lowest (6.7%)

Multivariate Analysis

Correlation Analysis

The correlation analysis reveals strong relationships between key operational variables. The most notable finding is the near-perfect correlation (0.998) between licensed buses and operational buses, indicating highly efficient fleet management. Additionally, moderate to strong correlations exist between route distance, operation time, and total trips, suggesting that longer routes naturally require more operational resources.

Key Variable Relationships

Bus Count vs Route Distance

Analysis shows distinct patterns across bus types. Trunk lines demonstrate the highest variation in both fleet size and route distance, reflecting their role as the backbone of Seoul's public transport system. Express buses, while fewer in number, tend to operate on the longest routes (average 54.1 km), serving inter-city connections.

Total Trips vs Operation Time

A positive correlation exists between operation time and total trips, with trunk and feeder lines showing the highest daily trip frequencies. Customized buses exhibit the lowest trip frequency, consistent with their on-demand service model.

Operational Efficiency Analysis

Fleet Utilization

The analysis reveals exceptional fleet utilization across all bus types, with an overall correlation of 0.998 between licensed and operational buses. This indicates that nearly all licensed vehicles are actively deployed, minimizing idle capacity. The small gap between licensed and operational buses (average 0.73 spare buses per route) demonstrates efficient resource allocation while maintaining necessary backup capacity.

Service Frequency Optimization

Service intervals vary systematically by bus type and route characteristics. Trunk and feeder lines maintain the shortest intervals (8-12 minutes minimum) during peak hours, while express and night buses operate with longer intervals (12-20 minutes) reflecting different demand patterns. The relationship between route distance and service interval shows a clear positive correlation, suggesting optimized scheduling based on operational constraints.

Spare Vehicle Management

Spare vehicle ratios vary significantly by bus type, reflecting different operational requirements. Customized buses maintain the highest ratio (40.8%) due to the critical nature of their specialized services. Trunk lines operate with minimal spare capacity (4.4%), relying on predictable demand patterns and robust maintenance schedules. This differentiation demonstrates sophisticated resource planning adapted to service characteristics.

Recommendations

Operational Optimization

  1. Consider dynamic fleet allocation strategies for trunk lines during peak periods to address the 6-10 minute interval variations observed in the data
  2. Evaluate opportunities to increase night bus frequency on high-demand routes, particularly those showing consistent ridership patterns
  3. Review spare vehicle ratios for customized buses to identify potential efficiency gains without compromising service reliability

Resource Planning

  1. Implement predictive maintenance scheduling based on the strong correlation between route distance and operation time to minimize unplanned downtime
  2. Explore opportunities for inter-type fleet sharing during off-peak hours, particularly between feeder and trunk lines
  3. Consider gradual expansion of express routes to suburban areas showing population growth, based on the successful utilization patterns of existing routes

Data-Driven Decision Making

  1. Establish real-time monitoring systems to track the efficiency metrics identified in this analysis continuously
  2. Develop benchmarking frameworks comparing operational performance across similar route types to identify best practices
  3. Integrate passenger demand data with operational metrics to optimize service frequency and route planning dynamically

Appendix

Methodology

This analysis employed multiple statistical and visualization techniques to examine relationships between operational variables:

  • Correlation analysis to identify relationships between continuous variables
  • Distribution analysis using box plots and histograms to understand variability
  • Multivariate scatter plots with categorical grouping by bus type
  • Efficiency metrics calculation (trips per bus, spare vehicle ratios)
  • Comparative analysis across bus types and operating companies

Data Quality Notes

The dataset was thoroughly cleaned and validated before analysis. All numeric variables were checked for consistency and outliers were examined in context. The high correlation between licensed and operational buses (0.998) serves as a validation check, confirming data accuracy and consistency.

Limitations

This analysis represents a snapshot as of December 2025 and does not capture temporal variations throughout the year. Seasonal patterns, special events, and weekday/weekend differences are not reflected in this cross-sectional analysis. Future studies should incorporate time-series data to understand dynamic operational patterns.
